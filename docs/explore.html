Analytical unit

The study in plain terms

  • The place. The Ogun River drains about 22,000 km² of southwest Nigeria, past Abeokuta, into the Lagos Lagoon.
  • The question. When it rains more than usual, does more of the river's floodplain go under water, and does that muddier water reach the lagoon?
  • How. Free satellite data, every month from 2019 to 2024: rainfall, soil wetness, radar images of flooded ground, and how cloudy the lagoon water looks.

    Methods, audit & limitations

    How this was measured

    1. Geographical Separation: Catchment Sub-Basins vs. Estuarine Zone

    Units Z1–Z4 represent topographic sub-basins delineated from WWF/HydroSHEDS/v1/Basins/hybas_9 within the Ogun River drainage system. Each sub-basin is traced upstream through the HydroBASINS NEXT_DOWN network from a fixed level-9 outlet unit (Oyan Dam for Z2, the upper Ogun above the Oyan confluence for Z1, the lower mainstem at about 6.9°N for Z3), with the reaches below that forming Z4. The catchment is the 22,166 km² drainage area above the Ogun mouth. Unit Z5 (Northwest Lagos Lagoon Estuary) is an estuarine receiving water domain where open-water pixels (JRC occurrence ≥ 50%) are analysed for spectral turbidity (NDTI). Catchment and estuarine units are not interchangeable.

    2. Sentinel-1 SAR Inundation Proxy & Permanent Water Exclusion

    Sentinel-1 C-band GRD (IW, VV, ASCENDING pass; the descending track covers only ~5% of the catchment) scenes are filtered with a 50 m focal-median kernel. A pixel counts as flooded when VV < -16.5 dB, it is at least 3 dB darker than its January–March dry-season reference, Copernicus GLO-30 Slope < 5.0°, JRC water Occurrence < 80% and MERIT Hydro height above nearest drainage < 15 m. A plain threshold was dominated by dry-season false alarms (burnt savanna and dry ground in Z1–Z2), so the catchment series is measured over the lower-Ogun floodplain (Z3–Z4). Monthly median composites miss short floods and C-band radar cannot see water under vegetation, so this is an uncalibrated inundation index rather than a validated flood map.

    3. Sentinel-2 Spectral Turbidity Indicator (NDTI) & Missing Monsoon Months

    Sentinel-2 L2A scenes are masked using the Scene Classification Layer (SCL) to remove clouds, cirrus, and shadows, and restricted to open water (MNDWI > 0 and SCL == 6). NDTI = (B4 − B3)/(B4 + B3) is a relative spectral proxy for water optical properties and is not calibrated to NTU or gravimetric Total Suspended Solids (mg/L). Months with fewer than 100 valid cloud-free water pixels in the estuary are preserved as null (NaN) and never replaced with zero or interpolated prior to statistical testing.

    4. Per-Pass Detection, Optical Cross-Check & Robustness

    Flooding is detected on every Sentinel-1 pass; the monthly value is the mean flooded area (each pixel's fraction of flooded passes × its area), and the maximum extent is also exported. As an independent check, Google Dynamic World (Sentinel-2) pixels labelled water or flooded vegetation are measured over the same floodplain. The two do not agree (see Validation in the audit table): Dynamic World has 40–60 clear images a month in the dry season but only 1–3 in the wet season, so its signal is dominated by cloud availability, while the radar index retains some dry-season false alarms. The rain–inundation association is also tested with each month left out in turn; it depends strongly on the October 2024 flood. Treat the SAR series as an unvalidated index.

    5. Oyan Dam Regulation

    The Oyan–Ofiki tributary (Z2) is regulated by the Oyan Dam, whose releases can drive lower-Ogun flooding independently of rainfall. Release records are not publicly available, so the monthly Sentinel-1 open-water area of the Oyan Reservoir is exported as a regulation proxy and partialled out of the rain–inundation association. If release records are added (--dam-releases), they are partialled out as well.

    6. Statistical Analysis & Temporal Autocorrelation

    All correlations in src/02_compile_observatory.py are computed on pairwise complete observations, reporting both raw seasonal associations and deseasonalized anomaly associations, Bretherton autocorrelation-adjusted effective sample sizes (n_eff), and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR-adjusted p-values across lags τ ∈ {0, 1, 2} months.

    7. Known Limitations

    Cloud-masked NDTI months cluster in the wet season, so SAR–NDTI associations are weighted towards dry-season conditions. Six years give only six values per calendar month for deseasonalising. Tides, dredging, sand mining and urban runoff also affect lagoon turbidity and are not modelled. No in-situ flood extents or gravimetric TSS samples were available for validation.
    About this observatory

    How to use this site

    This observatory tests whether rainfall and shallow soil moisture across the roughly 22,200 km² Ogun River catchment relate to floodplain inundation, and whether that flooding reaches the northwest Lagos Lagoon as turbidity. All data come from one Google Earth Engine export (2019–2024) and are recomputed by the repository's own scripts.

    Using the site

    1. Overview gives the headline numbers and the findings, each with a verdict.
    2. Map: switch indicators at the top left; click a unit to open it. Units drawn hatched with a dashed outline have no reliable value for that indicator.
    3. Unit shows one unit's measurements. Time series shows 72 months as four aligned charts; hover to read the same month across all of them.
    4. Statistics is the full audit table. Compare puts two units side by side.
    5. Share copies a link to exactly the view you are looking at, or prints it. Data → Map image and Download charts (Time series) save figures as PNG.
    6. By month on the map steps through 2019–2024; Seasonal cycle in Time series shows the average year.
